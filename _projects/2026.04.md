The Erdős–Szekeres convex polygon theorem states that for any positive integer $$N$$, every sufficiently large finite set of points in general position in the plane contains a subset of $$N$$ points that form a convex $$N$$-gon. This study proves the Erdős–Szekeres convex polygon theorem from the perspective of Ramsey theory, compares it with geometric proofs, and analyzes its efficiency, including the upper bound on the number of points, while exploring possibilities for improvement and seeking a novel proof. In particular, we verify the existence of a convex $$n$$-gon using $$R_4(n, 5)$$ and subsequently investigate proofs that enhance efficiency by employing other Ramsey numbers. This research holds significance in analyzing and advancing the limitations of existing proofs, thereby contributing to a deeper understanding of the interplay between geometry and combinatorics.
