This study analyzes the Weyl algebra A₁ = ⟨x, ∂ | ∂x − x∂ = 1⟩, the algebraic structure behind the Heisenberg uncertainty relation, and shows how its representation theory and center change with the characteristic of the base field.

In characteristic 0, a trace argument proves that A₁ has no finite-dimensional matrix representation: if AB − BA = Iₙ, then Tr(AB) − Tr(BA) = 0, while Tr(Iₙ) = n, forcing n = 0, a contradiction. The center of A₁ is then just the scalars. In characteristic p, this obstruction vanishes since Tr(Iₚ) = p ≡ 0 (mod p), and we construct an explicit 3×3 representation over F₃ verifying DX − XD = I₃. We also show xᵖ and ∂ᵖ become central, expanding the center to F_p[xᵖ, ∂ᵖ].

We link this to the Jacobian conjecture. It remains open in characteristic 0, but in characteristic p we give a counterexample: f(x) = x − xᵖ satisfies f′(x) = 1 yet is not injective, by Fermat's little theorem. Finally, we discuss how the enlarged center in characteristic p bridges the Dixmier conjecture and the Jacobian conjecture, linking operator algebra and algebraic geometry.
